E.2 Methodological Context
All models were tested under consistent conditions:
• Use of publicly accessible standard interfaces
• No API-level access or parameter tuning
• No system prompt modification beyond the defined experimental frame
• Interaction via standard chat interfaces
In contrast to Stage 1 (formal constraint testing), Stage 2 introduces instructional variation across three levels:
• L1 – Explicit rule hierarchy (deterministic prioritization)
• L2 – Semantic goal framing (implicit prioritization)
• L3 – Multi-level hierarchical structure (explicit but complex prioritization)
Each model was evaluated across all three levels using identical decision scenarios.
The objective was to measure frame persistence under varying degrees of explicitness and structural complexity.
E.3 Important Interpretation Note
The results of this study are:
• not a performance ranking of models
• not a benchmark comparison
• not an assessment of general intelligence or capability
The study exclusively evaluates:
→ decision persistence across instruction types
→ stability of priority structures
→ susceptibility to drift or reinterpretation
Observed differences between models should therefore be interpreted as:
→ differences in decision architecture and frame handling
and not as “better” or “worse” performance.
E.4 Limitations
The following limitations apply:
• Public model versions may change over time due to updates
• Some systems (e.g., Perplexity, Copilot) integrate multiple underlying models
• Internal model parameters and routing mechanisms are not transparent (black-box condition)
Additionally:
• Results reflect behavior under natural interaction conditions, not controlled inference environments
• Instruction interpretation may vary depending on internal alignment layers or safety mechanisms
The study therefore captures:
→ observable behavior under realistic usage, not internal model logic
E.5 Purpose of Disclosure
The full model mapping is provided to ensure:
• transparency of the evaluation setup
• reproducibility within publicly accessible environments
• comparability across future SFP studies
It enables subsequent research to:
• replicate the conditions
• extend the framework
• test additional models under identical structural constraints
