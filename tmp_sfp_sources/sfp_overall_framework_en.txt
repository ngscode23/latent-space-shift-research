SFP OVERALL FRAMEWORK
Overview Document
Project: AIReason
Date: 21 March 2026
Author: Juliane Schubert
1
Background
The SFP study (System Frame Persistency) investigates the extent to which large language models
maintain predefined decision frames stably across multiple steps.
The focus is not on the quality of individual responses, but on the structural capacity of models to
consistently reproduce a priority logic once it has been established.
2
Objectives
The overall study examines three core aspects:
· Persistence: Do decision rules remain stable across sequences?
· Drift: When and how do deviations occur?
· Priority Architecture: How do models weight competing requirements?
The study does not follow a benchmark approach, but rather a behavioral-analytic perspective on
LLM systems.
3
Study Structure
The SFP study consists of two interrelated sub-studies:
SFP-1 (Alpha / F-Test)
Focus: Fundamental capacity for frame stabilization under explicit conditions.

· Simple, clearly defined rules
· Direct instruction binding
· Baseline for persistence behavior
Objective: Determining whether models are capable of maintaining explicit frames in a
stable manner.
SFP-2 (L-Study: L1–L3)
Focus: Variation of frame structure and its influence on decision stability.
· L1 – Explicit rule (formal priority)
· L2 – Semantic goal frame
· L3 – Hierarchical priority structure
Objective: Analyzing how different frame formulations affect persistence and drift.
4
Shared Methodological Approach
All parts of the study follow a unified test logic:
· identical decision scenarios (8 items)
· binary decision structure (stable vs. short-term)
· multiple runs per model
· evaluation exclusively at the decision level
Metrics used:
· Persistence Length (PL)
· First-Break Position (FBP)
· Persistence Rate (PR)
Note: Justifications were not evaluated — only the decisions themselves.
5
Central Research Question
The overarching research question is:
→ Under what conditions do LLMs interpret frames as binding rules — and when are
they relativized or overridden?
6

Meta-Findings (across SFP-1 and SFP-2)
The combined analysis reveals three fundamental patterns:
1. Explicit rules are unstable
→ Models rapidly lose formal specifications under generation pressure
2. Semantic frames are highly stable
→ Goal descriptions often exert stronger influence than explicit rules
3. Hierarchies are error-prone
→ Models remain stable, but partially follow incorrect priorities
7
Key Finding
The primary problem is not insufficient stability, but rather:
→ stable, but displaced priority weighting
This suggests that models possess implicit decision logics that can override explicit specifications.
8
Relationship Between Sub-Studies
SFP-1 and SFP-2 are not independent — they build upon each other:
· SFP-1 provides the baseline for frame stability
· SFP-2 shows how this stability changes across different frame types
Together, they constitute an investigation into how decision frames emerge, operate, and evolve in
LLMs.
9
Classification
The SFP study does not primarily understand LLMs as text generators, but as:
→ dynamic decision systems
The findings are therefore relevant to:
· Alignment research
· Agent design
· Safe AI systems
· Prompt and frame architectures

10
Purpose of This Document
This document serves as a shared framework for:
· SFP-1 (Alpha / F-Test)
· SFP-2 (L1–L3)
It ensures that both studies remain:
· conceptually connected
· methodologically consistent
· jointly interpretable
